# The California Hearing Aid Dispenser Practical Exam: research notes

Steve asked (2026-10-28) for "an exhaustive research into the california hearing aid dispenser practical exam" so
Booth Camp can build its own practical exam in the Training room. This page is that research. The game's design is in
[PRACTICAL_EXAM.md](PRACTICAL_EXAM.md).

**How to read it.** Every claim has a source number in square brackets (section 11) and the date that source carries.
All sources were read on **2026-10-28**. Marks:

- **[V]** verified: read in the source's own text.
- **[S]** from a tool summary of a long PDF (the fetch tool returns about the first 39,000 characters verbatim and
  summarises the rest): probably right, not checked word for word.
- **[U]** unverified: one secondary source, a conflict between sources, or our inference.

Nothing here reproduces board exam content beyond short quotes for citation. The board does not publish the
examiners' score sheets, station cards or pass mark, and the game must not pretend to have them.

## 1. At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Who runs it | The Speech-Language Pathology and Audiology and Hearing Aid Dispensers Board (Department of Consumer Affairs) [1][3] [V] |
| Who must pass it | Every hearing aid dispenser applicant, and dispensing audiologists who want to fit aids; written exam first [3][4] [V] |
| Shape | **Two timed stations on a live subject**: Station A, ear inspection and audiometric assessment, **35 min**; Station B, ear impression, evaluating instruments, fitting, post-fitting, **30 min** [1] [V] |
| Day length | "a minimum of three (3-4) hours"; no breaks in the testing area [1] [V] |
| Scoring | Two examiners per room, scripted prompts on cards, criterion-referenced pass mark set with the Office of Professional Examination Services (OPES); the mark is not published [1][7] [V] |
| Fee | $500 for the practical (non-refundable); $300 application + written for a new dispenser applicant [3][8] [V] |
| Frequency, place | At least twice a year by law, "on average three to four times"; five dates were listed for 2026; "currently ... only being offered in Sacramento" [1][3][4] [V] |
| Pass rate (recent) | fiscal 2024-25: 73 % overall, **64 % first-time** [23]; June 2025: 34 of 50 (68 %); January 2026: 38 of 50 (76 %) [12][13] [V] |
| Biggest safety rule | **Safe Support Technique** for anything put in or near the ear; an unsafe task is stopped and may not be finished [1][6] [V] |
| Results | in writing, "six (6) to eight (8) weeks" (bulletin) or "four to six weeks" (website) [1][3][4] [U: the sources disagree] |

## 2. The path to the exam

- **Who can apply.** At least 18, high school or equivalent, fingerprinted, and must "Pass California Hearing Aid
  Dispensing Written and Practical Examinations" [3] [V]. "You must pass the written exam before taking the practical
  exam" [3][4] [V]. Business and Professions Code 2538.25 requires both exams [10][14] [V, as quoted in board minutes].
- **Three routes** [3] [V]:
  1. Written exam applicant without a licence: may not work until licensed.
  2. **Trainee** with a supervisor licensed at least three years in California: a six-month temporary licence,
     renewable twice; the written exam must be taken within the first 10 months.
  3. **Licensed in another state:** a 12-month temporary licence, not renewable; fail either exam and the licence is
     surrendered at once.
- **No training hours are required** before the exams: "Current regulations do not require trainees to complete a
  specific number of hours of training/supervision before taking the written and practical exams" (Aug 2018) [10] [V].
  The board discussed a required course of instruction in 2019 [15][16] [V]; we found no adopted requirement [U].
- **Filing.** Apply with the fee during the posted filing period; mail that arrives before the first day is returned.
  An Exam Security Agreement and a **Subject Application** must come back by the date given, or the candidate cannot
  sit and the fee is kept [1] [V]. Applicants are "accepted in the order received", and seats depend on examiners,
  location and health and safety (May 2022 committee) [17] [V].
- **2026 dates listed** (location column blank): Jan 30-31, Mar 20-21, Jul 24-25, Sep 18-19, Nov 6-7; no date after
  November 2026 was posted when we looked [3] [V]. Earlier: April 21, July 21 and October 2018 [10][11] [V/S]; April 6
  and July 27, 2019 [15] [V]; June 13-14 and September 26-27, 2025 [12] [V].

## 3. The two stations

The bulletin (March 2025) is the candidate's guide [1] [V]. It names six content areas split over two stations:

| Station | Time | Content areas |
|---|---|---|
| **A: Ear Inspection and Audiometric Assessment** | 35 min | I Ear Inspection, II Audiometric Assessment |
| **B: Procedures for Evaluating Hearing Instruments and Ear Impressions** | 30 min | III Ear Impression, IV Evaluating Hearing Instruments, V Fitting, VI Post-fitting |

### 3.1 Station A: ear inspection and audiometric assessment (35 minutes)

"Candidate establishes pure tone air, pure tone bone thresholds, and uncomfortable listening level on a human
subject" [1] [V]. What the bulletin says is assessed ("focus of assessment") [1] [V]:

| Focus | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Room and equipment preparation | preparing the audiometer; the **listening check** |
| Otoscopic inspection | otoscopic technique; **hygienic precautions** |
| Audiometric thresholds | national standards for pure-tone thresholds; unmasked thresholds; masked thresholds by the **"Plateau" method**; pure-tone uncomfortable listening level (threshold of discomfort, TD); recording thresholds |

- **Candidate brings:** otoscope, bone oscillator, clinical audiometer, **non-insert** headphones [1] [V].
- **Station provides:** audiograms and red, blue and black pens [1] [V].
- The area assesses the ability "to evaluate the external ear to determine if the client's need for a medical
  referral and determine the client's hearing ability from pure tone tests" [1] [V].

**The threshold of discomfort (TD) procedure** the bulletin suggests ("only suggestions") [1] [V], in our words:

1. Seat the subject so the candidate **can see their face the whole time**.
2. Start at the **highest frequency tested**, at **70 dB**.
3. Present tones in **pairs of short bursts**, at least one second between pairs.
4. Raise **no more than 5 dB** between pairs, up to the instructed level or the audiometer's limit.
5. Instruct the subject to raise a finger when it gets annoying and the hand when they do not want to listen any more.
6. **Watch for reflexes** (eye blink, change of expression, head jerk).
7. Record like a threshold, marked **TD**; no response at the limit = an arrow down at the 110 dB line, marked TD.

A separate board PDF, "Proper Positioning for Threshold of Discomfort Test", exists but is pictures only; the fetch
returned its title alone [6] [V: title only]. **Steve: a copy would help (section 10).**

### 3.2 Station B: impressions, instruments, fitting, post-fitting (30 minutes)

"Candidate performs an otoscopic inspection, produces a finished ear impression on a subject, identifies conditions
suitable for ear impression, evaluates hearing instrument(s), evaluates and fits new hearing instrument, and
identifies source of client complaints with the hearing instruments. **Only one ear impression will be taken**" [1]
[V]. The bulletin's table, flattened by the PDF extraction, so the row pairing is inferred [1] [V text, U pairing]:

| Focus | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Otoscopic inspection | otoscopic technique; hygienic precautions |
| Health conditions suitable for impressions | conditions for **referral**; conditions **suitable** for an impression |
| Preparation for the impression | **selecting the ear dam**; inserting and placing it |
| The impression | preparing, inserting and removing the material; recognising a **well-formed impression** |
| RIC delivery and orientation | fitting tubing in earmolds; fitting the RIC to the ear and orienting a new user; the delivery process; **realistic expectations**; care and maintenance |
| Replacing tubing on earmolds | the retubing procedure (station provides **pre-bent #13 tubing**) |
| Troubleshooting | physical inspection; listening check; testing the instrument; correcting problems; which **electroacoustic parameters** a fault affects; service |

- **Candidate brings:** otoscope, ear light, ear dams in several sizes, impression material (in date), a stethoscope
  or other listening device, and **a clear syringe with a clear non-detachable tip or a non-electric impression gun**
  [1][5] [V].
- **Station provides:** scissors, blower bulb, batteries, battery tester, #13 tubing, and a tool tray (blunt
  scissors, tubing stretchers / expanders / pullers, needle-nose pliers, screwdriver, twist reamer, brush, pipe
  cleaners, wax pick / loop, cotton swabs, emery board, black felt pen, magnifier) [1] [V].
- "Candidate must **fully express** ear impression material to the end of the syringe/gun tip prior to ear
  impression" [1] [V]; "There should be no air in the tip of the syringe or gun" [6] [V].
- What the bulletin does **not** mention: rechargeables, telecoil, feedback by name, real-ear or test-box
  verification, fitting software, speech tests [1] [V: absent from the text we read]. The 2020 outline does list
  rechargeables, telecoil, feedback and electroacoustic analysis as knowledge [2] [V], so they may come up as
  questions [U].

### 3.3 Safety and hygiene

- **Safe Support Technique** [1][6] [V]: any instrument in or near the ear (otoscope, ear light and tips, otoblock,
  syringe or gun). Hands, head and instrument move as **one unit**; at least one hand always touches the head; one
  hand is the **bridge** on the side of the head, the other holds the instrument steady against the cheek or the
  bridge hand. "When done safely, the instrument will move with the head and not into the ear" [1] [V]. The board's
  photo sheet shows: otoscope handle up, otoscope handle down, two-finger support on a syringe or gun, support for
  otoblock placement (pull the pinna up, a finger on the cheek) [6] [V: captions].
- **Unsafe = stop.** "If an examiner believes that the candidate is performing a task in an unsafe manner, they will
  be asked to stop. The candidate may not be allowed to complete the task" [1] [V]; the candidate goes on to the rest.
- **Hygiene:** "hygienic precautions" is a focus in both stations [1] [V]; the outline's first area includes
  "Sanitize equipment ... before contact with client" [2] [V]. Candidates bring hand sanitizer (10 oz or less) and
  **non-bleach** wipes [1] [V]. No more detailed infection-control rule is written in the bulletin [1] [V].
- **Equipment rules:** own equipment, "no sharing", audiometer calibrated within 12 months with matching papers,
  supplies in clear plastic bags [1] [V].

### 3.4 The subject

The candidate brings their own subject, approved by the board beforehand [1][3] [V]: 18 or older with photo ID; not a
subject for this exam in the past year (nor for a year after); no dispensing or audiology licence, manufacturer or
dispensing-company job, or exam-coaching link in the past 10 years; a clean canal and no medical ear problems, which
the candidate checks in the days before [1] [V]. The subject signs that the board is not responsible for the
candidate's negligence [1] [V].

## 4. What the exam measures: the 2020 outline

The practical outline (2020) [2] [V] comes from the 2020 occupational analysis [9] [V/S]: "six nonweighted content
areas" [9] [V]. Our paraphrase of the tasks (the outline also lists knowledge statements under each):

| # | Area | Tasks (paraphrased) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Equipment / pre-visit | check function and calibration; sanitize equipment before contact; keep a room fit for testing; listening check |
| 2.1 | Pre-assessment | ear inspection and otoscopy; decide on a **physician referral** from symptoms, signs and history (including the **laws** on referral) |
| 2.2 | Assessment | explain the test; pure-tone **air and bone**; **masking** (incl. under- and over-masking; masking for speech is listed as knowledge); **threshold of discomfort** (TD / UCL / LDL); chart and document |
| 3 | Ear impression | judge whether an impression is safe; tell the client what they will feel; pick and place the **block** (cotton dam), check it with the otoscope; fill canal and concha; check the cure; **break the seal**; remove; **re-inspect** the ear; compare the impression with the ear (anatomical landmarks, enough for manufacturing; mastoid cavities named as abnormal anatomy) |
| 4 | Fitting and delivery | insert the aid and judge fit; teach insertion and removal; controls, phone use, assistive devices; **batteries** (use, care, disposal) and **rechargeables**; care and maintenance; features, accessories, **telecoil** |
| 5 | Follow-up / post-fitting | judge complaints and performance (**internal vs external feedback**, electroacoustic analysis to spec, volume control, hearing change vs fault); maintenance and repair incl. **earmold tube replacement**; repair in office, send away, or replace |
| 6 | Counselling | **realistic expectations**, adaptation, factors in success (cognitive and physical); communication strategies in different places |

- The occupational analysis: survey letters to 1,157 dispensers and 1,172 dispensing audiologists (Nov 2019), 285
  usable responses; 13 subject-matter experts in October 2019 and January 2020 workshops; a practical cut-off rating of
  3.0; the impression tasks became their own area [9] [V]. "Take digital scan of ear canal" was rated **below** the
  cut-off, so digital scanning is not on the practical [9] [S].
- The outline's area 2 includes speech masking as knowledge, but neither station lists speech testing [1][2] [V]. We
  read the practical as **pure tones + TD only** [U].
- The previous occupational analysis for dispensers was in 2012 [10] [V].

## 5. Scoring and passing

- Two examiners per room; they "must adhere to the script"; prompts are written on cards; no conferring; a candidate
  who knows an examiner is moved [1] [V].
- OPES and licensee subject-matter experts develop the exam; the pass mark is **criterion-referenced** (minimum
  competence), the same standard for every candidate [7] [V]. "A licensing examination determines whether an examinee
  is competent to practice, not whether the examinee performs better or worse than others" [1] [V].
- The **pass mark, the item list and whether a station can be failed on its own are not published** [1][2][7] [V:
  absent]. Whether one unsafe act fails the whole exam is not stated [U]; the bulletin says only that the task stops.
- **Appeals** within 60 days, only for examiner misconduct or a significant procedural error (16 CCR 1399.122) [1] [V].
- **Retakes:** a failing candidate receives a retake application [1] [V]; the 2022 rule change set the retake fee
  equal to the first sitting after an OPES review [17] [V/S]. No retake limit is published [U].
- **Examiners** are California licensees with at least five years in practice (workshops three), with a résumé and
  three letters; the recruitment form asks about syringe and gun comfort and how often they mask [18] [V, form
  revised 2016]. Examiners can earn continuing education for exam days (proposed 2022: up to 2 hours a day, 4 per
  renewal) [17] [V].

## 6. Pass rates and how it has changed

| When | Candidates | Passed | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feb 2018 (committee concern) | | "fifty percent of candidates fail" | [11] [V, no data shown] |
| Nov 2018 (OPES at the board) | | "19 percent failure rate" | [19] [V, no data shown] |
| June 13-14, 2025 | 50 | 34 (68 %); dispenser trainees 17 of 23; dispenser applicants without supervision 0 of 2 | [12] [V] |
| Jan 30-31, 2026 | 50 | 38 (76 %); dispenser trainees 23 of 30; without supervision 1 of 4 | [13] [V] |

The 2018 numbers disagree with each other [U]. The two recent sittings say roughly **a quarter to a third fail**, and
supervised trainees pass far more often than unsupervised applicants (small numbers) [12][13] [V; the comparison is
ours].

**Changes over time:**

- 2012: the previous dispenser occupational analysis [10] [V].
- 2016: examiner recruitment form revised [18] [V].
- 2018: the board voted to drop the practical for **dispensing audiologists** on OPES's advice (Aug, 5-1); a motion to
  reconsider failed in November [10][19] [V]. Later fee schedules still list a $500 practical for dispensing
  audiologists [8] [V], so how this ended is unclear [U].
- 2019: the board weighed the IHS national **written** exam (44 states use it) and a required training course; both
  went to committee [15][16] [V].
- 2020: new occupational analysis and the current two-station outline with the separate ear-impression area [2][9]
  [V].
- 2022: the bulletin's April 2022 revision; exam regulations 1399.120-1399.122 amended (order received, seat limits,
  retake fee, removal for cheating) [1][17] [V/S].
- March 2025: current bulletin, the same two stations and times [1] [V].
- **July 17, 2026:** the International Hearing Society (IHS) presented its **national practical exam** to the board:
  six sections (otoscopy and ear impression 15 %, audiometric evaluation 25 %, audiometric interpretation 20 %,
  selection, fitting and follow-up 20 %, verification 10 %, troubleshooting 10 %), 105 scoring elements worth 1-4
  points, one overall pass mark, scored on the spot, live subject for audiometry, mannequin allowed for impressions
  and verification, $200 a test; 39 states require a practical and 16 use the IHS one [20] [V; S for the candidate
  guide in the same packet]. IHS noted California covers referral law and assistive-device teaching, which IHS does
  not, and IHS covers interpretation and verification, which California does not [20] [V]. **We found no decision**;
  the meeting's minutes were not online when we looked [21] [U]. This is the change to watch: if California adopts
  it, real-ear or test-box verification and audiogram interpretation join the exam.

## 7. Common failure points

The board publishes **no failure analysis** [U]. What the sources point to, strongest first:

1. **Unsafe instrument handling.** The only rule that stops a task; it is repeated in both stations and has its own
   photo sheet [1][6] [V]. No bridge hand, the instrument not braced, the hand leaving the head.
2. **The impression.** It is its own content area since 2020 [9] [V]; one impression only [1] [V]; the material must be
   fully expressed with no air [1][6] [V]; the dam has to be checked with the otoscope and the ear re-inspected after
   [2] [V]. An impression that misses landmarks or has voids cannot be "well-formed" [1][2] [U: our inference].
3. **Masking.** Masked thresholds by the plateau method on a live subject inside 35 minutes, with under- and
   over-masking named as knowledge [1][2] [V]. Examiners are asked how often they mask [18] [V].
4. **The TD procedure.** A specific suggested routine with a face-visible seat and a recording rule [1] [V].
5. **Skipping a step:** the listening check, sanitizing, re-inspecting after the impression, recording [1][2] [V that
   they are assessed; U that they are commonly missed].
6. **Supervision matters:** unsupervised dispenser applicants passed 1 of 6 across the two recent sittings, trainees
   40 of 53 [12][13] [V numbers; U on the cause].

Candidate accounts and prep courses add nothing more specific (section 8); the one commercial course gives
"Otoscopy & Bracing" its own module [28] [V], which agrees with point 1.

## 8. Secondary sources (candidate accounts, prep courses)

Very little first-hand material is on the open web; we found **no** candidate post that describes the stations or
names failure points [U: absence]. Reddit could not be searched from the container (the proxy refused it), and
Facebook groups are not indexed. What exists:

- **Senate Business and Professions Committee, sunset background paper (March 2026)** [23] [V]: in fiscal 2024-25 the
  practical had "177 first-time test takers with a passage rate of 64%" and "an overall pass rate of 73%"; first-time
  rates were not tracked before then; the $500 fee dates from Feb 1, 2011 and a $100 rise is under consideration; the
  exam "requires actual demonstration of the knowledge and techniques"; committee staff asked the board to explain
  "why the first time and overall pass rates ... are so low." The paper does not mention the IHS practical.
- **IHS California page** (undated) [24] [V]: "This licensing agency does not use IHS' Practical Exam".
- **IHS Practical Examination Candidate Guide, 2023** (the national exam; hosted by Ohio) [25] [V]: names
  "Otoblock selection and placement", masked and unmasked pure tones **and speech**, infection control, verification,
  and troubleshooting (six questions and three broken aids). Useful as the shape California may move towards.
- **One candidate account** on HearingTracker (2019) [26] [V]: a Costco trainee passed the written in April and the
  practical in August, after IHS coursework and a year in an audiology practice. No station detail.
- **A San Francisco clinic blog** (Jan 2022) [27] [V]: "a rigorous, hands-on test ... under the watchful eye of a group
  of proctors"; lists ear inspection, audiometric assessment, impressions, repairing aids, fitting and post-fit
  counselling.
- **A commercial video course** (Alan Lowell Seminars, undated, not California-specific) [28] [V]: about 7 hours;
  modules include "Otoscopy & Bracing", masking, bone conduction, ear impressions, "Tube Change for BTE's & Earmolds",
  service and performance, counselling and delivery. The closest thing to a practical-prep outline we found.
- **University of the Pacific AuD handbook** (2020-21) [29] [V]: AuD students sit the California dispensing exams as a
  cohort, the practical in spring, before their externship. AuD students are a large share of candidates [U].
- **Other states** for comparison: Massachusetts weighed the IHS practical in 2023 and asked for "identification of red
  flag situations" to be added [30] [V]; Wisconsin runs its own "Audiometric and Ear Mold Practical Examination" [31]
  [V].
- Written-exam prep sites (PrepPool, YouTube practice tests, Hearing Aid Academy) cover the **written** exam only
  [32] [V]. The Krumenacker *Hearing Aid Dispensing Training Manual* (2nd ed., 2019) is a general practical-exam aid
  [33] [V: publisher page only].

**Updated pass-rate picture:** fiscal 2024-25 overall 73 %, first-timers 64 % [23]; June 2025 68 %, January 2026 76 %
[12][13]. Roughly **one first-time candidate in three fails**.

## 9. Medical referral (the law the exam cites)

The outline's pre-assessment area includes "laws and regulations pertaining to signs and symptoms that require a
medical referral" [2] [V]. California's list (Business and Professions Code 2538.36(a)) [22] [V], paraphrased: before
fitting, suggest in writing a visit to an ear physician when there is

1. a visible congenital or traumatic deformity of the ear;
2. drainage from the ear, now or in the past 90 days;
3. sudden or rapidly progressive loss in the past 90 days;
4. acute or chronic dizziness;
5. a one-sided loss of sudden or recent onset in the past 90 days;
6. a significant air-bone gap;
7. visible significant wax or a foreign body in the canal;
8. pain or discomfort in the ear.

These are the same eight red flags the game's `CaseRules` already teaches [U: to be checked line by line in the
build PR].
